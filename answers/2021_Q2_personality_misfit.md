# 2021 Q2: Personality Misfit — Socialisation vs ASA Theory

**Reusability tags:** `personality`, `Big Five`, `extraversion`, `introversion`, `personality misfit`, `person-organisation fit`, `socialisation theory`, `ASA theory`, `attraction-selection-attrition`, `Schneider`, `personality stability`, `personality faking`, `Barrick and Mount`

## Question

An employee, Joon, was recently hired in the sales department of a company. His job duties require a high level of extroversion, and thus, the sales department has a culture that values extroversion. According to his honest reports on the Big-five personality test, Joon has a high level of introversion. Based on this information, you would like to predict how Joon will react to and resolve this misfit. Discuss two competing theories that provide opposite predictions regarding Joon's behaviour.

*Note that whenever you draw on a theory, you should explain the basic tenets of the theory.* [100%]

---

## Full Essay Answer

Joon's situation represents a classic case of **personality misfit**: his underlying personality profile — specifically, high introversion — is at odds with both the requirements of his sales role and the culture of his department, which values extroversion. Extroversion is characterised by sociability, assertiveness, and positive energy in social situations; these are the very traits required to visit customers, persuade them to purchase products, and maintain close social relationships. To predict how Joon will react to and resolve this misfit, we first need to consider whether personality change is a realistic option, before turning to two competing theories: **Socialisation Theory** and **Attraction-Selection-Attrition (ASA) Theory**.

### Why Personality Faking Is Not Applicable

One might first ask: can Joon simply "fake" extroversion on the job? Organisational Behaviour research strongly suggests that this is not a viable long-term solution. As established in the study of personality (Robbins & Judge, 2024), personality traits are **enduring characteristics** that remain relatively stable over time and across situations. Personality is primarily determined by genetic and hereditary factors, with research on twins raised apart consistently demonstrating that a substantial proportion of personality variation is attributable to genetics. While personality has been shown to stabilise by early adulthood, short-term faking of personality — sustained performance of personality traits one does not naturally possess — places a significant psychological burden on the individual and is unlikely to be sustainable.

Furthermore, Joon's introversion score was reported **honestly** on the Big Five personality test, reflecting his genuine, stable personality rather than a temporary state. This establishes his introversion as a stable trait, making deep personality change (or sustained faking) highly unlikely. The question is therefore not whether Joon will change his personality, but how he and the organisation will resolve the misfit.

### Theory 1: Socialisation Theory — Prediction: Joon Adapts

**Core Tenets of Socialisation Theory:**

Organisational socialisation is the process by which employees are adapted to the culture of the organisation (Robbins & Judge, 2024). Through socialisation, the culture of an organisation progressively makes employees more similar to one another in terms of their values, assumptions, beliefs, and behavioural norms — and potentially in terms of personality expression. Socialisation can be understood as occurring in three stages:

1. **Prearrival:** The beliefs, values, and attitudes that the individual brings with them before joining the organisation, shaped by prior experience and education.
2. **Encounter:** The new employee confronts the reality of the organisation, and begins to reconcile their prior expectations with the actual culture, norms, and demands they encounter on the job.
3. **Metamorphosis:** The employee internalises the organisation's values, norms, and cultural assumptions, increasingly adopting behaviours and attitudes consistent with the organisational culture.

**Prediction for Joon:**

Under Socialisation Theory, the sales department's culture — which strongly values extroversion — will exert sustained social pressure on Joon to adapt. Through daily interactions with extroverted colleagues, exposure to extroverted norms and expectations, and the social reinforcement of extroverted behaviour, Joon may gradually begin to exhibit more extroverted behaviours on the job. While his underlying personality may remain introverted, he may adapt his behavioural expression to align with the cultural demands of the department, achieving a degree of fit through acculturation.

Socialisation Theory therefore predicts that **Joon will resolve the misfit by adapting himself to the organisational culture** — the outcome being accommodation rather than exit.

### Theory 2: Attraction-Selection-Attrition (ASA) Theory — Prediction: Joon Quits

**Core Tenets of ASA Theory:**

Attraction-Selection-Attrition (ASA) Theory, developed by Schneider (1987), proposes that people and organisations are continuously evaluating fit between individual personalities/values and organisational cultures. The theory identifies three mechanisms:

1. **Attraction:** Individuals are attracted to organisations whose cultures, values, and climate align with their own personality and values. They self-select into environments that feel right for them.
2. **Selection:** Organisations select candidates whose personality and values fit the organisational culture. Selection processes — both formal (interviews, assessments) and informal (manager intuition) — tend to favour those who fit.
3. **Attrition:** When fit is poor — either because the individual was not well-selected, or because they or the organisation have changed — the individual is more likely to quit (or to be pushed out). Over time, this cycle produces **relative personality homogeneity** within organisations, as those who do not fit tend to leave.

**Prediction for Joon:**

Under ASA Theory, Joon's high introversion in an extroversion-dominant culture represents a significant misfit. He will be continuously evaluating the discrepancy between his personality and the cultural expectations around him. This misfit is also likely to impair his job performance: research by Barrick and Mount (1991) established through meta-analysis that **extraversion is a particularly strong predictor of performance in sales roles**, which require the very social skills and energy that extraverts naturally possess. Joon, as a high introvert, is therefore likely to underperform in his role.

As performance suffers and discomfort with the cultural misfit grows, the probability of attrition increases. ASA Theory therefore predicts that **Joon will resolve the misfit by leaving the organisation** (or being managed out), rather than by adapting.

It is worth noting that ASA Theory builds on person-organisation (PO) fit theory but goes further: PO fit describes the degree of compatibility between individual and organisation, while ASA Theory specifically explains the dynamic process by which misfit is resolved through attrition over time.

### Additional Nuances and Qualifications

Several factors might moderate which theory's prediction is more accurate:

1. **Difficulty of personality change favours ASA:** Because personality is primarily genetic and stabilises by early adulthood, the depth of behavioural adaptation that Socialisation Theory requires may be unrealistic for a high introvert. The more stable an individual's personality, the more likely that attrition — rather than adaptation — will be the outcome.

2. **Openness to Experience as a moderator:** An individual high in Openness to Experience (another Big Five dimension) is more likely to embrace novel experiences, adapt to new environments, and experiment with unfamiliar behaviours. If Joon is also high in Openness, he may be more likely to adapt (favouring Socialisation Theory). If he is low in Openness, attrition becomes more probable.

3. **If Joon is in a leadership role:** Leaders are not merely recipients of organisational culture — they actively shape it. If Joon were to attain a leadership position, he might not need to adapt or quit; instead, he could reshape the culture of the department to be more tolerant of introversion. However, given that Joon was "recently hired" in the sales department, this scenario is unlikely in the near term.

### Conclusion

In summary, two competing theories offer opposite predictions about how Joon will resolve his personality misfit. Socialisation Theory predicts that exposure to and pressure from the extroverted culture will gradually lead Joon to adapt his behaviour to fit organisational norms. Attraction-Selection-Attrition Theory predicts that the persistent misfit between Joon's introversion and the department's extroverted culture will lead to declining performance and eventual attrition. Given the fundamental stability of personality as a trait and the strong performance implications of extroversion in sales roles, ASA Theory may ultimately offer the more probable prediction — though the outcome will depend on individual and contextual moderators.
